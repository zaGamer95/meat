Chicken

Chicken

The cheapest meat at the counter and the easiest to ruin. Most of the decisions happen before cooking — how the bird was chilled, and whether you buy it whole.

Air-chilled vs water-chilled

After slaughter, birds have to be cooled fast. How that is done changes what you take home.

Water-chilledAir-chilled
MethodImmersed in chilled chlorinated waterCooled by cold circulating air
Absorbed waterUp to ~8% of weightNegligible
SkinWaterlogged; fights browningDry; crisps readily
PriceLower — but some of it is waterHigher per pound, denser meat
If the skin matters, it is worth the premium. Failing that, leave a water-chilled bird uncovered on a rack in the fridge overnight — the surface dries out and browning improves dramatically.

Temperature

165 °F / 74 °C is the safe minimum everywhere on the bird, dark meat and white. But the two do not want the same finish.

Breast is lean and turns chalky past 165 °F, so it wants to arrive there and stop. Thigh and drumstick carry connective tissue that keeps improving to 175–185 °F — they get more tender, not less. Cooking a whole bird is really the problem of getting both right at once, which is what spatchcocking solves.
