Pork

Pork

Modern pork is far leaner than the pork the old rules were written for, which is why so much of it comes out dry. Almost everything worth knowing follows from that one fact.

The temperature everyone still gets wrong

In 2011 the USDA lowered the safe minimum for whole pork cuts from 160 °F to 145 °F / 63 °C with a 3-minute rest — the same as beef and lamb. Trichinosis in commercial pork has been effectively eliminated.

145 °F pork is faintly pink in the middle, and that is correct. Most people cooking pork to leather are following a rule that was retired over a decade ago. Ground pork is the exception — still 160 °F.

Two kinds of pork

The split that matters is lean-and-fast versus fatty-and-slow. Treating one like the other is the usual failure.

CutCharacterMethod
Loin, chops, tenderloinVery leanFast, hot, pulled at 145 °F. Brine helps.
Shoulder / Boston buttFat and collagenLow and slow to 195–205 °F, until it gives.
BellyFat in layersSlow render, then crisp hard at the end.
RibsCollagen on boneLong and gentle; bend test, not a clock.
Two targets, not one. 145 °F is for lean cuts you want juicy. Shoulder and ribs go well past it on purpose — you are not chasing doneness there, you are melting collagen into gelatin, and that needs hours above 160 °F.
