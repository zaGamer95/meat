Buying & cooking

Meat, without the guesswork

Most meat goes wrong before it reaches the pan — the wrong cut bought for the wrong method. These notes cover what to look for at the counter, which cut suits which kind of heat, and the handful of temperatures actually worth memorising.

By animal

Each one is bought differently. Beef is about grade and primal, pork about keeping a lean animal from drying out, chicken about how the bird was chilled and broken down.

Safe internal temperatures

These are food-safety minimums, not doneness preferences. Measure at the thickest part, away from bone. Doneness ranges for steak live on the beef page.

USDA safe minimum internal temperatures.
WhatTemperatureNotes
Beef, pork, lamb & veal — steaks, chops, roasts 145 °F / 63 °C Then rest 3 minutes before carving.
Ground beef, pork & lamb 160 °F / 71 °C Grinding spreads surface bacteria throughout.
All poultry, whole or ground 165 °F / 74 °C Thigh and breast both.
Leftovers & casseroles 165 °F / 74 °C Reheat throughout, not just at the edges.
Carryover cooking is real. A thick roast keeps climbing 5–10 °F after it leaves the heat. Pull it early and let the residual heat finish the job — waiting for the target temperature in the oven means overshooting it on the board.

Storage

Fridge at or below 40 °F / 4 °C. Freezer times are about quality, not safety — frozen meat stays safe indefinitely, it just stops being good.

CutFridgeFreezer
Steaks, chops & roasts3–5 days4–12 months
Ground meat1–2 days3–4 months
Whole chicken1–2 days~12 months
Chicken pieces1–2 days~9 months
Cooked meat3–4 days2–6 months

Thawing

In the fridge, overnight — slow, safe, and the meat stays in one piece. In cold water, changed every 30 minutes, if you forgot. Never on the counter: the outside sits in the danger zone for hours while the middle is still frozen.
